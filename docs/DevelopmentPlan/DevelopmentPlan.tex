\section*{About This Document}

This document records how Team 11 will build \progname{}: how we meet and
communicate, who is responsible for what, how code and documents move
through git, when major pieces of work happen, how the Proof of Concept
(PoC) demonstration will test our biggest risk, and which tools we expect
to use. The problem and goals themselves are described in the Problem
Statement and Goals document. This plan is written for two audiences: the
current team, as the reference for how we agreed to work, and viewers of
the document, such as the teaching assistant (TA), the instructor, and
peer reviewers, who
should be able to read it and understand how the project is run without
asking the team directly. Decisions recorded here can change; changes are
made through a pull request that updates this document.

Table~\ref{TblTerms} defines the terms and acronyms used in this
document. Each acronym is also spelled out where it is first used.

\begin{table}[H]
\caption{Terms and acronyms} \label{TblTerms}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lX}
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
\midrule
LLM & Large language model.\\
Prefill & The first phase of LLM inference, in which the model processes
the whole prompt at once.\\
Decode & The phase after prefill, in which the model generates output one
token at a time.\\
KV cache & The keys and values computed for each prompt token during
prefill, stored so they do not have to be recomputed during decode.\\
Trunk & The part of a prompt shared by several agents (for example, a
document all agents read).\\
Branch & The part of a prompt specific to one agent (its instructions).\\
Tree attention mask & An attention mask that lets several branches share
one trunk in a single forward pass while never seeing each other.\\
vLLM & An open-source LLM serving engine that Tandem will extend.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\section{Team Meeting Plan}

\paragraph{When and where.} The team meets once a week for up to one hour,
on Mondays from 6:00 to 7:00 pm, separate from the Wednesday TA-team
meeting slot. Meetings alternate between in person on campus
and online through a Microsoft Teams call, so that at least every second
meeting is in person. Physical meeting locations are shared in the team
chat, not in this public repository. When a lecture or tutorial slot has
nothing scheduled, the team uses it as an extra working session. In the
week before a deliverable is due, the team adds a second short check-in.

\paragraph{Agenda and minutes.} Every team meeting has a GitHub issue,
created from the \texttt{Team Meeting} issue template at least 24 hours
beforehand. The chair writes the agenda in the issue. The standing agenda
is:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item Review action items from the last meeting.
  \item Blockers: anything stopping someone's work.
  \item Status of the next deliverable and the checklist items it still
  needs.
  \item Decisions needed this week.
  \item New action items, each with one owner and a due date.
\end{enumerate}
During the meeting Granola records notes and a summary automatically; the
chair ticks off attendance in the issue and turns that summary into
decisions and action items as comments. Every action item becomes its own
issue, assigned to its owner, and the meeting issue is closed once those
issues exist.

\paragraph{Chair.} The chair rotates weekly in alphabetical order by last
name. This gives every member the chair role several times each term.

\paragraph{Supervisor meetings.} We do not yet have a supervisor; the team
is contacting potential advisors, who do not need to be at McMaster. Once
one is confirmed, we plan to meet every two weeks on Microsoft Teams,
tracked with the \texttt{Supervisor Meeting} issue template.

\section{Workflow Plan}
\label{sec:workflow}

This section is written to become the project's \texttt{CONTRIBUTING.md}.

\subsection{Branches and Commits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{main} is protected: nothing is pushed to it directly, and
  every change arrives through a pull request (PR).
  \item Work happens on a short-lived branch named
  \texttt{type/short-description}. The type is one of \texttt{feat},
  \texttt{fix}, \texttt{docs}, \texttt{poc}, \texttt{bench}, or
  \texttt{chore}.
  \item Commits are small and focused, with a message in the imperative
  mood that explains why the change was made, not just what changed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pull Requests}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Every PR links the issue it resolves (``Closes \#12''), describes
  what changed, and says how it was tested.
  \item A PR needs one approving review from another team member and
  passing CI before it is merged. Work in progress is opened as a draft PR
  so others can see it early.
  \item Reviewers check correctness and readability, and for documents,
  the matching checklist in \texttt{docs/Checklists/}.
  \item PRs are merged with ``squash and merge'' so each PR becomes one
  commit on \texttt{main}, and the branch is deleted afterwards.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tags}

When a deliverable is submitted, the commit on \texttt{main} that was
submitted is marked with an annotated tag (for example, \texttt{PS-DevPlan},
\texttt{SRS-HA}, \texttt{PoC}, \texttt{Rev0}, \texttt{Final}). This lets
the TA, the instructor, and peer reviewers see exactly what was graded, and
lets the team compare later work against it.

\subsection{Issues}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Every piece of work, bug, research question, and meeting is an
  issue. The repository already has issue templates for team meetings, TA
  meetings, supervisor meetings, lectures, and peer reviews.
  \item Each issue gets one \emph{type} label and, for code, one
  \emph{area} label:
  \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Type: \texttt{bug}, \texttt{enhancement}, \texttt{documentation},
    \texttt{research}, \texttt{meeting}, \texttt{review}, \texttt{question}.
    \item Area: \texttt{area:core}, \texttt{area:engine},
    \texttt{area:measurement}.
  \end{itemize}
  \item Each issue has one assignee and belongs to a milestone. There is
  one milestone per course deliverable, with the deliverable's due date.
  \item Peer review issues from other teams (label \texttt{review}) are
  answered within a week and closed with a link to the commit that
  addresses them, or a short explanation if no change is made.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Continuous Integration}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Documents (already running):} a GitHub Actions workflow
  (\path{.github/workflows/latex-pages.yml}) rebuilds every changed
  document, commits the PDFs to \texttt{pdfs/}, and publishes them to the
  project's GitHub Pages site.
  \item \textbf{Code (added with the first code PR):} a second workflow
  runs the linter and the unit tests on every PR. This workflow includes the
  tree
  attention correctness test with a small model on the central processing
  unit (CPU), so a change that
  breaks the mask fails CI before it is merged.
  \item \textbf{GPU work:} GitHub's CI machines have no GPU, so GPU tests
  and benchmarks are run by hand on the department clusters, and their
  results are committed to the repository with the command used to produce
  them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proof of Concept Demonstration Plan}
\label{sec:poc}

The PoC demonstration tests the two things Tandem's thesis depends on:
that the problem is real in today's serving engines, and that our core
mechanism is correct. It does not build the fix inside vLLM; that is the
work planned after the PoC.

\subsection{Risks}

The PoC is planned around the risks most likely to stop the project:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The problem may already be solved (thesis risk).} Tandem
  assumes that when several agents with the same long shared context
  (trunk) arrive at a serving engine at the same time, the engine
  processes the trunk once per agent, because its prefix cache is still
  empty. If current versions of vLLM already avoid this, most of the
  project's benefit disappears.
  \item \textbf{Correctness of the core mechanism.} Tandem depends on
  evaluating a shared trunk and several branches in one forward pass with a
  tree attention mask, and getting \emph{exactly} the same result as running
  each branch on its own. If a branch can see another branch, or tokens
  after itself, or gets the wrong positions, the results are silently wrong
  and nothing built on top can be trusted.
  \item \textbf{Learning curve and hardware access.} No team member has
  worked on an LLM serving engine before, vLLM is a large codebase, and
  realistic measurements need GPUs on the department clusters, which the
  team has not yet used.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the Demonstration Will Show}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Risk 1: measuring the gap in unmodified vLLM.} We run the
  current release of vLLM, with prefix caching turned on, on a department
  GPU cluster. A script sends $K$ requests that share one trunk and differ
  only in a short agent-specific branch, in three scenarios:
  \begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \emph{Simultaneous arrival:} all $K$ requests are sent at once,
    with nothing cached.
    \item \emph{Warm cache:} the trunk is processed once first, then the
    $K$ requests are sent.
    \item \emph{Staggered arrival:} the requests arrive a few milliseconds
    apart.
  \end{enumerate}
  For each scenario, and for several values of $K$ and trunk length, we
  report the time to first token (TTFT) and the number of prompt tokens
  vLLM actually processed (taken from its metrics, not estimated). Since
  vLLM's paged KV cache uses a fixed number of bytes per token, prompt
  tokens processed is also a direct stand-in for KV cache memory: fewer
  tokens computed means less KV cache memory allocated. If simultaneous
  arrival processes close to $K$ copies of the trunk while the warm cache
  processes one, the gap Tandem targets is real, and the difference
  between the two scenarios is the most Tandem can save. Reporting actual
  GPU memory usage and KV cache read/write throughput directly, rather
  than deriving them from token counts, is a backlog item for later
  benchmarking once the measurement moves past this PoC scale.
  \item \textbf{Risk 2: tree attention correctness.} A PyTorch program
  packs a shared trunk and several branches into one sequence, builds the
  tree attention mask and per-branch position IDs, runs one forward pass,
  and compares every branch's output scores (logits) against running that
  branch alone. The comparison covers the next token and several generated
  tokens, and must match to within floating-point rounding. The mask is
  printed so the structure can be checked by eye.
  \item \textbf{Risk 3: learning curve and hardware.} Running and measuring
  vLLM for Risk~1 requires installing it on a department cluster and
  reading enough of its scheduler and cache code to explain the result.
  The team will also present a short plan for the vLLM changes (which
  parts of the engine change, and why), showing that we understand where
  the work lies.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{If the PoC Fails}

If the Risk~1 measurement shows that vLLM already avoids most of the
repeated work, the team will review the thesis with the TA before building
anything, for example by focusing on the single-pass tree prefill alone.
If the tree attention mask cannot be made correct, or its overhead removes
any benefit, our fallback is to narrow the scope
to an \emph{observability} tool: instrumenting an existing serving engine
to detect and report repeated prefill work across concurrent multi-agent
requests, without necessarily removing it. This stays in the same problem
area, so work done before the PoC is not wasted. If that also proves too
ambitious, we would narrow further, for example to offline analysis of
recorded multi-agent prompts. This fallback was discussed with the
instructor during project approval, who agreed it is a sufficient exit
strategy; the exact rescoping would be decided with the TA after the PoC
demonstration.

\end{enumerate}

\section*{Appendix --- Team Charter}

This charter follows the structure of the University of Portland Team
Charter template.

\subsection*{External Goals}

Build something substantial enough to be a genuine portfolio and interview
talking point in systems engineering, and aim for a strong final grade;
present at the Capstone EXPO if the results are compelling.

\subsection*{Attendance}

These rules cover team meetings, TA meetings, and demonstrations.

\subsubsection*{Expectations}

Team members are expected to attend all scheduled team meetings on time and
stay for the full meeting; if unable to attend, notify the team as far in
advance as possible.

\subsubsection*{Acceptable Excuse}

Illness, other course or exam conflicts, and emergencies are acceptable
excuses. Simply forgetting, or deprioritizing without advance notice, is
not.

\subsubsection*{In Case of Emergency}

Notify the team chat and the meeting chair as soon as possible; the chair
will share Granola's meeting summary with the absent member to bring them
up to speed on decisions and action items.

\subsection*{Accountability and Teamwork}

These expectations apply to every member, whatever their current role.

\subsubsection*{Quality}

Members are expected to come to meetings having completed their agreed-upon
work, or to flag blockers before the meeting rather than at it. Pull
requests should be self-reviewed (tests passing, documents updated) before
requesting review.

\subsubsection*{Attitude}

We adopt the
\href{https://www.contributor-covenant.org/}{Contributor Covenant} as our
code of conduct. Disagreements are expected to be resolved through
discussion and, if unresolved, a team vote (see Decision Making below).

\subsubsection*{Stay on Track}

We use the course's project management metrics (commits, issue activity) as
a lightweight check-in signal, with a quantified trigger: if a member has no
commits or issue activity and misses two consecutive weekly meetings
without an acceptable excuse (see Attendance above), the team will raise it
with them directly in the next meeting. If it persists for a further two
weeks, the team will escalate to the TA.

\subsubsection*{Team Building}

The team eats together after milestone deliverables (Problem Statement,
PoC demo, Rev 0 demo, Final demo) to mark the milestone and decompress
before the next one starts. Weekly meetings open with a few minutes of
non-project conversation before the agenda starts, so meetings stay a
place the team wants to show up to, not just a status check.

\subsubsection*{Decision Making}

Default to consensus; if consensus is not reached after discussion, decide
by majority vote among team members.
